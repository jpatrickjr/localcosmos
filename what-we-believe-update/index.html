The problem

Local is fragmented.

A community can be described in hundreds of places.

  • Events
  • Businesses
  • Destinations
  • Organizations
  • Social media
  • Calendars
  • Directories
  • Spreadsheets
  • GIS
  • Institutional knowledge

But very little of it is connected.

Everyone sees part of the place. Nobody sees the whole place.

The real problem

The hardest problem isn't storing local information. It's knowing when reality changed.

  • A business opened.
  • Hours changed.
  • An event moved.
  • Ownership changed.
  • A restaurant closed.
  • A new destination appeared.

Local information begins aging the moment it is published.

What Local Cosmos is

What if a community could continuously understand itself?

Local Cosmos is an activation company for local economies.

We are building systems that help communities:

  1. 01Understand what is here.
  2. 02Know what is changing.
  3. 03Improve what people discover.
  4. 04Turn information into participation and economic activity.

Better local information creates better local experiences.

Local Operating Information System

Meet LOIS.

LOIS is the intelligence layer behind Local Cosmos.

It is designed to help communities discover change, establish trusted local information, understand relationships, and activate that knowledge across local experiences.

  1. 01Detect

    Something may have changed.

  2. 02Verify

    Is it true?

  3. 03Organize

    Update the trusted record.

  4. 04Connect

    Understand what it affects.

  5. 05Activate

    Put that intelligence to work.

Built close to the ground

You can't understand local from a dashboard alone.

Local Cosmos is being shaped through fieldwork, conversations, observation, and early pilot exploration with the people who actually manage and experience local communities.

One recurring challenge has become especially clear

Keeping local business information current across systems is still highly manual.

  • Businesses open.
  • Close.
  • Move.
  • Change owners.
  • Change hours.
  • Change services.

And someone eventually has to notice.

Those conversations are shaping the first use cases for LOIS.
